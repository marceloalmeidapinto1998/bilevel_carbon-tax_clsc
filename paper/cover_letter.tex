\begin{letter}{The Editor\\ European Journal of Operational Research}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit the manuscript \emph{Carbon taxation with anticipated network redesign: a mixed-integer bilevel model for closed-loop supply chains} for consideration as a Theory and Methodology paper in the European Journal of Operational Research. We propose \emph{OR in environment and climate change} as its first keyword.

The paper asks how a regulator should set a carbon tax when the operator responds by redesigning a multi-period closed-loop supply chain, with supplier selection, facility location, production and remanufacturing technologies and intermodal transport. We formulate the question as a bilevel program with a continuous tax and a mixed-integer follower, and make three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item We show that an optimal tax exists and lies at the lower end of the tax range or at one of the operator's switching prices, the break points of its value function, and that a common scaling of the emission factors divides these prices by that factor while the tax bill as a share of cost is unchanged.
\item We give two exact methods and show when each applies: enumeration of the switching prices, which returns all of them when the operator's problem can be solved to optimality, and an adaptation of the deterministic bounding procedure of Soares, Alves and Henggeler Antunes (2021, EJOR 291, 52--66) to a relaxation in which the tax multiplies a continuous emission aggregate, which needs few iterations whatever their number and still bounds the optimum when the operator's problem cannot be solved to optimality.
\item On instances calibrated to the case study of Mota et al. (2018, Omega 77, 32--57), operators have a median of 11 switching prices; a fixed tax at the 2025 EU allowance price overpays where the optimal tax lies below it and abates less where it lies above, and the tax acts through remanufacturing and cleaner technology.
\end{itemize}
The paper relates to recent EJOR work on bilevel optimisation with a sustainability perspective and on bounding methods for mixed-integer bilevel problems, and states its limits plainly: the tax is set for one operator, and the case study's optimal tax is not determined within its gap.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors approve its submission. The authors declare no competing interests. The code, instances and results will be made available as stated in the manuscript.
