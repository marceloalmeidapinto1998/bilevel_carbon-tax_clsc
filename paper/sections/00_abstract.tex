\begin{abstract}
We study closed-loop supply chain (\CLSC) network design under a
government-imposed carbon tax, formulated as a bilevel mixed-integer
program (\BLMIP) that makes the regulator--operator interaction
explicit. The regulator (leader) selects a discrete carbon tax,
lexicographically minimising the induced supply-chain emissions and
then levying the lowest tax that attains that minimum; the
supply-chain operator (follower) responds by optimising facility
location, capacity, technology selection, transportation, and forward
and reverse product flows to minimise operating cost inclusive of tax.
We solve the resulting \BLMIP with a \emph{deterministic bounding
procedure} (\DBP) that compute the regulator's optimum function 
between two bounds. A linear-programming (\LP) relaxation of the 
follower provides a lower bound, and the follower's exact integer 
response provides an upper bound. The \DBP iteratively tightens these 
two bounds until their gap is below $\epsilon$. Because the supply chain 
chooses among finitely many configurations, its emission response is 
piecewise constant and saturates: beyond the saturation threshold, 
further taxation abates nothing. A regulator targeting emissions alone 
is therefore  self-defeating --- prone to setting arbitrarily high taxes 
that yield no additional abatement. The saturation threshold is determined 
by the installed production and remanufacturing capacity of each network. 
Because that threshold differs across network designs, the tax varies 
by an order of magnitude across our computational instances, and no 
single carbon tax fits all.


\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
bilevel optimisation;\; carbon pricing;\; closed-loop supply chain network design;\;
environmental regulation;\; global optimisation;\;
lexicographic optimisation;\; mixed-integer programming.
\end{abstract}
