\section{Solution Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

We develop a \emph{Deterministic Bounding Procedure} (\DBP) that
solves problem~\eqref{eq:leader} to global $\epsilon$-optimality.
The \DBP maintains a lower bound $\LB$ on the leader's optimal
emissions and an upper bound $\UB$ attained by a bilevel-feasible
solution, tightening both iteratively until the optimality gap falls
below a prescribed tolerance $\epsilon$.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Lower-bound construction}
\label{sec:method:lb}

A valid lower bound is obtained from a \emph{relaxation master} that
optimises over the same feasible region $\setX$ as the follower
(\Cref{sec:model:follower}) -- crucially, $\setX$ does not depend on
$\taxindex$; only the follower's objective~\eqref{eq:follower} does.
Each time the follower is solved exactly at some tax index
$\taxindex^{(j)}$, obtaining the optimistic response
$\bz^{(j)}\in\Sol{\taxindex^{(j)}}$ with $c_{j}\defeq\Cost(\bz^{(j)})$
and $e_{j}\defeq\Emis(\bz^{(j)})$, the inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut}
\Cost(\bz)+\gridstep\,\taxindex\,\Emis(\bz)
\;\le\;
c_{j}+\gridstep\,\taxindex\,e_{j}
\end{equation}
is valid for \emph{every} $\taxindex\in\mathcal{T}^{\tau}$ and every
$\bz\in\setX$ (\Cref{lem:lb_validity}), because $\bz^{(j)}$ remains a
feasible -- if not necessarily optimal -- competitor in the
follower's problem at any tax index. After $k$ such evaluations have
accumulated the cut set $\mathcal{C}=\{(\taxindex^{(j)},c_{j},e_{j})\}_{j<k}$,
the relaxation master is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb_def}
\LB^{(k)} \;\defeq\;
\min_{\taxindex\in\mathcal{T}^{\tau},\,\bz\in\setX}
\Bigl\{\Emis(\bz)\ :\ \eqref{eq:cut}\text{ holds for every }
(\taxindex^{(j)},c_{j},e_{j})\in\mathcal{C}\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
$\LB^{(k)}$ is non-decreasing in $k$, since each new cut only shrinks
the master's feasible region, and bounds the leader's optimal
emissions from below. Both~\eqref{eq:cut} and~\eqref{eq:lb_def}
involve the product $\taxindex\,\Emis(\bz)$ of two of the master's own
decision variables; \Cref{app:impl_notes:unary} details its exact
\MILP linearisation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lb_validity}
For every $k\ge0$, $\LB^{(k)}\le\Emis(\bz^{*})$ for any optimal
$(\taxindex^{*},\bz^{*})$ of~\eqref{eq:leader}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
See \Cref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Upper-bound construction}
\label{sec:method:ub}

A valid upper bound is obtained by evaluating the follower exactly --
to \MILP optimality, with the optimistic tie-break of \Cref{as:opt}
-- at the sequence of tax indices $\taxindex^{(0)},\taxindex^{(1)},\ldots$
proposed by the relaxation master of \Cref{sec:method:lb}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Solve the follower \MIP~\eqref{eq:follower} at
        $\taxindex=\taxindex^{(k)}$ to optimality, obtaining
        $\bz^{(k)}\in\Sol{\taxindex^{(k)}}$.
  \item Compute $\Emis\!\bigl(\bz^{(k)}\bigr)$ via~\eqref{eq:emissions}.
  \item Set $\UB^{(k)}\defeq\min\{\UB^{(k-1)},\Emis(\bz^{(k)})\}$
        (with $\UB^{(-1)}\defeq+\infty$), updating the incumbent
        $(\taxindex^{\dagger},\bz^{\dagger})$ whenever the minimum
        strictly improves.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ub_validity}
For every optimal leader tax $\taxindex^{*}$ and every
$\bz^{*}\in\Sol{\taxindex^{*}}$, $\UB^{(k)} \ge \Emis(\bz^{*})$ for
every $k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate: $\UB^{(k)}$ is the emissions of an explicitly
bilevel-feasible solution, and $\Emis(\bz^{*})$ is optimal among such
solutions by \Cref{as:opt}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Convergence analysis}
\label{sec:method:convergence}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence of \DBP]\label{thm:convergence}
Under \Cref{as:det,as:lin,as:feas,as:opt}, the \DBP terminates in a
finite number of iterations and returns a solution with optimality
gap $\gap\le\epsilon$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
See \Cref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Iteration complexity]\label{rem:iter}
Each iteration evaluates one tax index exactly. Once cut~\eqref{eq:cut}
for index $\taxindex^{(j)}$ has been added, re-proposing
$\taxindex=\taxindex^{(j)}$ can no longer yield a relaxation value below
the optimistic value $e_{j}$ already found there (\Cref{app:proofs}).
Since $\mathcal{T}^{\tau}=\{\taxindexlb,\ldots,\taxindexub\}$ is finite, the
procedure therefore terminates after at most $\taxindexub-\taxindexlb+1$
iterations even at $\epsilon=0$, with no dependence on a refinement
step size and no breakpoint-density argument required.
\end{remark}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Leader tax tie-break}
\label{sec:method:tiebreak}

\Cref{eq:lb_def} minimises emissions alone; among master points tying
that minimum, an off-the-shelf \MILP solver may return any tax index,
not necessarily the smallest one the leader's lexicographic
objective~\eqref{eq:leader_obj} requires. Rather than searching for the
smallest tying tax index \emph{after} the \DBP loop of \Cref{alg:dbp}
terminates, we resolve the tie-break \emph{inside} the relaxation
master at every iteration: Stage~1 minimises emissions exactly as
in~\eqref{eq:lb_def}; Stage~2, restricted to master points within
numerical tolerance of that minimum, minimises $\taxindex$ itself. Both
stages re-use the same cut set $\mathcal{C}$, so this adds one cheap
re-solve of the master per iteration -- no separate phase, and no
additional MILP the size of the follower's.

\begin{lemma}[Tax tie-break validity]\label{lem:tiebreak_validity}
Fix iteration $k$ and let $\taxindex^{\star}$ be the smallest tax index
returned by the Stage-1/Stage-2 master of this section at iteration
$k$. If $\taxindex'\in\mathcal{T}^{\tau}$ is any tax index -- not
necessarily evaluated by iteration $k$ -- whose optimistic-optimal
response $\bz'\in\Sol{\taxindex'}$ attains $\Emis(\bz')=\LB^{(k)}$, then
$\taxindex^{\star}\le\taxindex'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
See \Cref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Once the surrounding \DBP loop's gap closes ($\LB^{(k)}=\UB^{(k)}=
\Emis(\bz^{*})$ for the leader's optimal $\bz^{*}$, by
\Cref{thm:convergence}), \Cref{lem:tiebreak_validity} applies with
$\taxindex'=\taxindex^{*}$ for every optimal leader tax $\taxindex^{*}$,
giving $\taxindex^{\star}\le\taxindex^{*}$; and $\taxindex^{\star}$ is itself
attained by a master point at emissions $\LB^{(k)}=\Emis(\bz^{*})$, so
$\taxindex^{\star}$ is itself an optimal leader tax, giving
$\taxindex^{*}\le\taxindex^{\star}$ by minimality of $\taxindex^{*}$ among
optimal taxes. Hence $\taxindex^{\star}=\taxindex^{*}$: the master's own
smallest tying tax index at termination is exactly the smallest tax
index in the \emph{entire} grid $\mathcal{T}^{\tau}$ attaining the
leader's optimal emissions -- not merely among the indices visited so
far -- with no monotonicity of $\Emis(\bz^{*}(\taxindex))$ in $\taxindex$
required.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{sec:method:implementation}

The \DBP is implemented in Python using \texttt{gurobipy}; the
reproducibility environment uses Python~3.14.4 and Gurobi~13.0.1. The
command-line entry point \path{tools/solver/solve_batch.py} exposes several
leader/follower oracles. The current implementation's lexicographic exact
workflow, \texttt{f\_lex\_exact}, is the full lexicographic leader
of~\eqref{eq:leader_obj} (minimise emissions, then minimise $\taxindex$,
\Cref{sec:method:tiebreak}) over a follower~\eqref{eq:follower} that is solved
to optimality with ties broken toward lower emissions (\Cref{as:opt}); the
tax tie-break is resolved inside the relaxation master at every \DBP
iteration, with no separate refinement phase. On the base instances the
workflow is cross-checked against an independent exhaustive enumeration of
the tax index, \texttt{enum\_lf\_lex\_exact}, which returns identical leader
decisions. (The results reported in \Cref{sec:experiments} were generated
under an earlier two-phase workflow, \texttt{lf\_lex\_exact} --- the \DBP of
\Cref{sec:method:dbp} followed by a separate bisection search for the
smallest tying tax index --- which \Cref{sec:method:tiebreak} has since
superseded; the numerical results are unaffected on the cross-checked base
instances, since both workflows returned identical leader decisions there,
but \Cref{sec:experiments} has not yet been regenerated under
\texttt{f\_lex\_exact}.)

The reported runs use relative \DBP tolerance $10^{-4}$, per-subproblem Gurobi
MIP gap $10^{-4}$, six solver threads, and no per-instance time limit; outputs
are written under \path{results/runs/} and every saved solution is revalidated
by an independent feasibility-and-objective check before being stored. We
therefore report each run as \emph{validated} at a relative gap below
$10^{-4}$: termination is by the relative-gap criterion of \Cref{alg:dbp}
rather than by an exactness certificate.
